What pricing it properly requires

Projected savings, then one fixed fee

The fee comes off the projected saving, not off your hours. Run that calculation on the planning work you already did this year and compare it to what you actually invoiced — the gap is the whole problem, and it is bigger than most owners expect.

Quote it once, in writing, before any work starts

The fee is owed whether or not they implement, and whatever the savings turn out to be. No adjusting afterwards, no savings guarantees, no money-back promises.

A separate engagement letter

Apart from return preparation. That one structural change is what makes the fee defensible, and it is the step most firms skip.

Keep it clean

This is value pricing, not a cut of the savings. A percentage-of-savings fee is a contingency arrangement, and your state board has opinions about those. Know exactly where that line sits before you quote anything.

Why this has to be now

Most planning moves have to be executed inside the tax year. Sell an engagement in November and your client sees the saving on the return you file in April. Sell the same one in February and the year is already written.

Fifteen hours of work is fifteen hours of work. What changes is what you write at the bottom of the letter.
