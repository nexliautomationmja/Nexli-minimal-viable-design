The part nobody says out loud

You already know which ones they are

The three emails about a $600 invoice. The shoebox in April. The ones who question your fee every year and have never once questioned your advice. They take the most and pay the least, and some of them are quietly failing.

You kept them because revenue feels like safety

Especially in February. So the list grows, the hours grow, and every year you promise yourself you will deal with it after busy season. You never do, because by May you are too tired to care.

Meanwhile the ones with real money at stake got nothing

Six and seven figures going to the IRS, sitting in your files, handled in the same rushed hour as a $400 Schedule C. Nobody ever offered them planning. Not because you could not do it — because there was no time left over to think about it.

To be fair about it

Nobody is telling you to fire half your clients next week. That is not a plan, it is a mood. The move is narrower and you can do it this afternoon: find the twenty who are worth a real conversation, and have that conversation before December 31. What you do about the bottom of the list gets much easier once the top of it is paying you properly.

Why this has to be now

Most planning moves have to be executed inside the tax year. Sell an engagement in November and your client sees the saving on the return you file in April. Sell the same one in February and the year is already written.

Next April looks exactly like the last one unless something changes before this year closes. Twenty names and one phone call is where it changes. Once those are paying you properly, replacing the bottom of your book stops being frightening — and that is a conversation for another day.
